Why this exists. If you read Google Maps in English, you see a fraction of what is written about these places. We measured it on this exact dataset: keeping only the English-language reviews removes 55% of the facts here, and the typical place drops from 8 known things to 4. Temperature and walking distance from the station vanish completely; roominess loses 97%, cleanliness 95%, toilets 94%, outlets 79%.

How it was made. 74 of 415 places had their Google reviews read by an LLM that extracts only physical-space attributes. Every value carries the sentence it came from, checked character-by-character against the original review — anything the model paraphrased is thrown away automatically.

Quotes are shown in the language they were written in. That is deliberate. A translated quote is no longer evidence, and the Korean sentence is the thing you would otherwise never see.

Each category has its own axes. Reading 548 restaurant reviews first, outlets, Wi-Fi, lighting and music came up zero times. Queue length, whether staff speak a foreign language, forced one-dish-per-person and high chairs did. So the tabs are split and the axes differ. A place that is both a cafe and a bar appears under both.

Only 22 restaurants so far. More once the scraping budget resets.

Only 3 bars, and no bar-specific axes yet. There was no sample of bar reviews to mine, so these use the restaurant axes. Things that matter in a bar — music volume, sitting without ordering food — are not here yet.

341 places are blank. Neighbourhood spots with only a couple of reviews, so there was nothing to extract. That is not a bug — it is half of this map, and only someone who has been there can fill it.

● is confidence. Using Google's own noise question to reviewers as ground truth, the average error with n observations came out around 1.81/√n.
